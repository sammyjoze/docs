Chatr's Changelog feature connects to your GitHub repository and turns commits into polished product update posts. Customers can see what's new directly in the chat widget — and you can keep them in the loop without writing a separate newsletter.

## Connecting GitHub

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Integrations">
    Go to **Integrations** in your Chatr dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect GitHub">
    Click **Connect** next to the GitHub section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authorize access">
    Authorize Chatr to access your GitHub account. You'll be redirected back to the dashboard once authorization is complete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select your repository and branch">
    Choose the repository and the branch you want to track — for example, `main`. Chatr will watch this branch for new commits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Initial sync">
    Chatr immediately syncs your recent commits and lists them as pending updates, ready for you to review and publish.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Publishing an update

Once GitHub is connected, Chatr surfaces new commits as draft posts you control before they go live:

1. Go to the **Changelog** tab under **Updates → Pending** to see commits awaiting review.
2. Review the auto-generated post — Chatr fills in the category, date, branch, and commit hash for you.
3. Click **Post Update** to publish it to the widget, or click **View on GitHub** to inspect the commit in full before deciding.
4. Published updates appear in the **Updates → Posted** list and become visible to visitors in the widget's **Latest updates** section.

## Adding updates manually

Click **Add Update** to write and publish a changelog entry without a GitHub commit. Use manual updates for announcements, pricing changes, policy updates, or any non-code news you want customers to see.

## What visitors see

Visitors tap **Latest updates** in the chat widget to browse a chronological feed of your recent product updates. This keeps your customers informed and engaged without requiring them to visit a separate blog or changelog page.

<Note>
  Only changes merged to your tracked branch (e.g. `main`) appear in the changelog. Commits on feature branches are not surfaced until they are merged.
</Note>
